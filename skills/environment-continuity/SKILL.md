# Environment continuity

Jason's standing direction, 2026-10-21: each scene environment needs NINE views arranged in ONE large 3×3 image artwork. Also retain every view as its own clean full-resolution image for video conditioning. A review webpage or nine unrelated settings does not fulfil the large-artwork requirement. This is nine views, not 81. Do not retrofit frozen approved packets automatically.

## Establish the location

Jason's21October2026 clarification: these are design/reference angles, not a storyboard or prescribed cameras for the finished film. The video director chooses framing, movement, staging, pacing and storytelling development autonomously. Plans/camera indices describe the reference artwork; do not turn them into mandatory shot order, lenses, screen direction or a required animatic. A rough spatial proxy, if useful, is editable and proposed, not an engineering survey or binding director plan.

Read the selected story, current art direction, accepted location master and user references. Assign a stable environment ID. Record the location's footprint, access points, levels, action route, persistent props, light sources and material/weather state in a simple plan. Mark invented or unresolved geometry as proposed; a rendered picture is not a measured building survey.

Choose nine useful views of the SAME location. Include its establishing view, reverse and opposing sides, action-critical approach/exit, an elevated view and overhead relationship where useful. Choose the actual cameras around the story; these examples are not a compulsory shot list. Nine views provide planned coverage, not proof of every possible camera angle. Subspaces needing their own coverage receive their own environment ID.

Inspect the first location master before propagation. Reference it and the plan for all later views; use the same physical anchors instead of independently inventing every angle. Preserve time, weather, light-source positions, facade rhythm, doors, windows, stairs, floor heights and objects. A reverse must reverse the camera, not mirror the building. New surfaces that were unseen in the master remain design proposals until reviewed.

## Deliver individual masters and one large sheet

Generate each view as a standalone clean image with no embedded panel border or caption. Preserve its native output and actual dimensions. Name it by environment and camera ID, such as `ENV01-01-south.png`. Keep approved individual art, including character front/profile/rear views, separately addressable; a collage alone is insufficient for identity or scene conditioning.

Assemble the nine selected images into one large raster artwork with exactly three columns and three rows. Use a deterministic layout renderer so the selected source pixels are preserved; do not ask a model to recreate approved photographs inside a collage. Do not stretch, silently crop or manufacture detail by upscale. State the sheet's actual pixel dimensions and each source's actual resolution. If the renderer pads cells, disclose the padding. Large canvas size is not a claim that each tile is native 4K.

Include a camera index mapping cells 01–09, in reading order, to individual files and plan positions. A review HTML sheet is additional navigation, not a substitute for the large PNG. Include the master, plan, all individual files, sheet and index in the self-contained folder with relative links. Keep exact approval scope and source hashes; revisions remain outside immutable snapshots.

## Review before handoff

Inspect every individual view, then compare the complete nine-view artwork. Check the same landmarks from opposite sides, gaps/clearances, doors and level changes, fixed equipment, floor/weather and motivated lighting. Check the action route against the plan. Record discrepancies rather than claiming automatic consistency from reference prompts.

Correct consequential continuity failures before marking coverage complete. Record actual missing cameras and resume those without repeating successful assets. Account/tool limits stop generation; no silent paid fallback. Agent review is not user approval and still art does not establish motion or engineering feasibility.

For the Shadow Apprentice only: 9:16 individual references; dry matte floors and surfaces, night photography; no rain, puddles or wet highlights. These are this film's locks, not a universal ban on water in future films. Preserve Aiko/Kaede accepted design while skin rendering remains under review.
