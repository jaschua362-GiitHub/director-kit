# Graphic spot method (one-prompt motion-graphics spot, Seedance 2.5 reference-to-video)

Adopted 19 November 2026 after TEST-029, the FAI reveal for Neo Siam. Jason's words: "this is more of a graphic spot, and then keep it as a skill learning tool, how to produce such graphic spot in the future." The technique was learned from a post by the X creator @Nexoxh (x.com/Nexoxh/status/2122963019365843424), who shared the prompt; credit that source when he says so. Evidence and his words: `lab file, not in kit: README.md`, its `DRAFT_D0x_REVIEW.md` files and `closeout-v01-DRAFT/DEBRIEF.md`, `TOKEN_REPORT.md`, `LESSONS_PROPOSED.md`.

**What it is:** a 15 s spot that is mostly graphic design in motion and a little character action: giant type, flat colour fields, hard cuts about once a second, one character moving through it. Seedance draws, cuts and scores it from one structured prompt and a small set of reference pictures. Exact type, the ending and the final sound are finished locally.

**What it cost the first time:** one day, USD 15.73 on Atlas, three draft takes, 262 M tokens. Run by this page it should be two drafts and about 120 to 150 M, with Fable at 25 to 35 M (an estimate, not yet measured).

**It does not replace the Lab's rules.** A quote he has seen and his typed yes before every paid job; copy approval before posting; the final delivery standard; the HD rule below.

## When to use it

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| A character reveal, a title spot, a pre-promotion for a film, a brand sting with one figure | This method |
| A story, acting, or a fight with real contact | The board route or the fight card |
| Type that must be exact in every cut | Not this: the model draws type about right; only local type is exact |

## Gate 0: the brief, before anything is made (Fable, one short session, free)

Ask all of this in ONE message, as a numbered list he can answer in one go. Every item here arrived late on TEST-029 and each late arrival cost a round or a take.

1. **Look:** is this part of an existing film's world? If yes, the film's palette, light and props are the starting point, never the reference's palette logic. Name the two or three colours and what each belongs to.
2. **Character:** which locked design, and how stylised (photoreal, game CGI, anime CGI)?
3. **Type:** which font, and is the file on this machine? Is an existing title lockup used or kept out?
4. **Words:** the name that must read, and where else the world's name must appear.
5. **Signature effects:** anything the film does that must look the same here (her blade's light, her powered eyes).
6. **Sound:** the take's own, the film's music, or a blend? Any voice?
7. **Length, format, ending:** 15 s, 16:9, and how it should end (settle and fade, or a hard cut to black).
8. **Deliverables:** poster cards and their formats, the platforms, the tone of the copy, who is credited, when it posts against the film.
9. **Quality and money:** his HD rule, and a cap for the piece.

Then put the look on ONE page (palette swatches, the font specimen, the character sheet's look, the title rule) and get his yes before any paid take. Reply with the plan, the gates and a token and money budget per gate.

## Gate 1: references (helpers build; the director passes each)

- **Character sheet** in the chosen rendering: front first (two looks if the rendering is new; he picks), then rear, profile, face, prop detail. A face made from a photoreal source comes back photoreal: make the face from the stylised front alone.
- **Type specimen:** every word the spot uses, each at the SAME large size, in the real font, light on dark. Words shown small get drawn in a default sans.
- **Colour card:** flat bands sampled from the film's own stills, no text.
- **Effect reference:** a tight crop of the film frame that shows the effect (the blade's light), no faces in it.

## Gate 2: the takes (Fable: the prompt and the verdicts)

The prompt, in this order (a working one: `lab file, not in kit: PROMPT_REVEAL_v04.txt`):

1. Format line: length, ratio, "exactly N distinct cuts", fps.
2. The ratio in words: "80% bold graphic design in motion and 20% character action".
3. Palette as rules: the ground colour, the graphic colours, what belongs only to the character, what never appears; point to the colour card.
4. Rendering of the character.
5. Reference assignment, her full description, "never redesign her".
6. The effect paragraph and the typeface paragraph, each pointing at its reference and saying the reference is never a frame of the film.
7. The timed cut list: one line per cut with timecodes, each a different layout idea.
8. Editing grammar, the complete list of words allowed on screen, the negatives.
9. Sound, with the peak named.

Rules learned the hard way:
- Never write a word with hyphens or slashes between its letters ("F-A-I" drew a dash). Spell letters with commas.
- A word the character crosses gets broken. A word that must read gets its own beat with no character in it, or she stays beneath it.
- Fewer things to get wrong: no numbers on screen unless needed; one word on screen at a time.
- Expect about three type slips a roll. Roll once, read every word frame by frame (a sheet every 0.25 s plus full-size frames wherever type is on screen), harden the prompt against exactly what slipped, roll once more. Keep one roll in reserve.
- Two rolls of nearly the same prompt share timecodes and layouts, so one cut swaps between them cleanly on hard cuts (drop or repeat frames evenly to fit; match black and white points by measurement). Do not design separate patch takes: they break the hand-overs between cuts.
- He judges motion and sound; the director sees frames. Say so each time.

Stop here: picture chosen. Write the one-page handoff and END the Fable session.

## Gate 3: the finish (an Opus 5.5 high session, to the director's written spec)

- **Local type:** set the name line in the real font. Its edges align to the big letters' edges by measurement, never by eye (his note: "typography alignment"). Give helpers a stated tolerance, or a half pixel stops them.
- **Ending:** the model stops mid-motion. Build it in the edit: either the last pulse reversed back to rest, a hold and a fade, or a hard cut to black on the pulse. Show both; he picked the hard cut.
- **Sound:** the sound of the take he likes can go on another take's picture by stretching it piece by piece to that take's cuts. A handover to the film's music needs the music heard earlier as single hits, a build of about four seconds, a designed hit, the take's sound faded over about a second, and an even level across it. A voice inside the take's music cannot be separated cleanly; plan around where it falls.
- **HD:** `draft-complete` finishes a whole take, never part of one, and redraws detail. Check it frame by frame against the draft and re-measure every overlay on the HD frames. His rule: native HD wherever her face is big or a still may be pulled; upscale elsewhere, accepted only after her face is checked at 100 %.
- **Master:** rebuild the locked draft at 1080p from a written build spec; verify frames, decode, loudness.
- Show him only the complete candidate. If a file is missing something, say what in the first words of the caption.

Fable returns for one short session: pass the master before he sees it.

## Gate 4: release and closeout (Opus for copy and closeout, Sonnet for posting)

- Poster cards: generate with the same references; the model draws only the short name; set the rest locally; check a 4:5 crop for the Reel cover.
- Copy matches the piece's tone (his words on this one: "more fun and mysterious ... surprise element"). Credit the source creator in his terms.
- Schedule on his approval of copy and time; read each post back on its platform.
- Debrief, token report, his messages word for word, lessons proposed for his ruling.

## Keeping tokens and scope in control

- **One Fable session per gate.** The cost on TEST-029 was the director's context (average 392 K, peak 676 K), not the helpers. At each gate: write the handoff, report tokens and money against the budget, stop. He opens the next session on the model named.
- **The parking list.** When he raises something outside the gate in hand, the director answers in one line, writes it on the parking list in the handoff (what, his words, which gate it belongs to), and does NOT start work on it. It is picked up at its gate. A change to something already locked is named as a change, with what it costs, before it is made.
- **One decision per message.** Each message to him ends with the single decision that gate needs. Other open items wait on the parking list.
- **Nothing downstream on an unlocked picture.** No sound, end line or posters until the take is chosen.
- **Helpers:** mechanical jobs (downloads, frame sheets, hashes, joins) go to Sonnet with exact paths and no card reading; judgement jobs to Opus high with a spec and pass or fail checks. Batch a gate's paid jobs into one quote. Helpers quote; the director makes the confirm call after his yes; a job that fails unbilled is a new quote and a new yes.
