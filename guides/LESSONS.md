2026-10-14 video lessons, DCL-20261013-SOLAR / TEST-007 (Claude Opus 5.5 + ChatCut Seedance 2.5). Evidence: experiments/TEST-007/RESULTS.md and COMPARISON_FILM01_vs_FILM02.md.

1. **Start and end frames from separate image edits cross-dissolve wherever their details differ.** Planters, steps and bushes ghosted mid-shot. Fix: build the end frame by compositing the new foreground over the start frame's exact background (`match_bg.py`).
2. **The endpoints pin incidental motion.** The cars drifted backwards in three takes because the end frame placed them further left. A prompt cannot override interpolation. Decide the direction of every moving element in the keyframes before submitting.
3. **The end frame locks the final acting.** The approved F03 pose made the women return to facing forward in every take. Eyelines and acting must be designed into the end frame (F03b/F03c).
4. **A start frame with different object geometry makes the model invent bridging motion.** The fan flipped edge-on and the light ran backwards. Derive the start frame from the end frame and change only what should animate.
5. **Image editors barely move the camera on request** (asked for a 12° orbit, got a few degrees). Don't plan an orbit on a derived offset frame.
6. **A pasted 2D cutout of a small object reads as fake:** no haze, no light change, a texture mismatch, a mask that clips the fan. Re-place the model's own per-frame renders instead (`carfix_native.py`). Better still, get the motion right in the keyframes (lesson 2).
7. **Portrait reframes:** hold → move → hold, plus reversals, felt abrupt, and a reframe across a cut read as a jump cut. Use an eyeline cut (show what the character looks at) and one continuous eased move per shot.
8. **Ops:**
   - Wait for a file newer than the submission; the first attempt copied the previous take.
   - Verify the encoder with ffprobe. This ffmpeg build has no libx264, and NVENC needs NVIDIA driver ≥610, so the real encoder was OpenH264 / h264_mf.
   - ChatCut Agent chat turns bill credits.
   - Typing prompts in 15-character chunks costs heavy screenshot tokens. Install the ChatCut Claude plugin.

## 2026-10-14 — video lessons, DCL-20261014-WEFT / TEST-008 (Claude Opus 5.5 + ChatCut plugin, Seedance 2.5)
Evidence: experiments/TEST-008/RESULTS.md and the review/ sheets.

1. **Derive the shot's start frame from its approved end frame, then lock the background.**
   - ECC-align the derived frame to the end frame.
   - Composite only the action region and face over the end frame's exact pixels (`build_b0c.py` / `build_b0d.py`).
   - Result: zero background ghosting in both S02 takes. This repeats TEST-007 lesson 1/4 with stronger evidence. It also helps that an image edit of the end frame usually keeps the framing (ECC 0.996).
2. **The start pose decides which limb the model animates.**
   - With the resting forearm lying across the body, away from where the end frame raises it, Seedance raised the *other* hand.
   - The correct hand then dissolved off the bench and the sleeves merged.
   - Rest the limb directly below its end position, so the move is a short lift about one joint, and name the single visible hand in the prompt.
3. **Image editors differ on pose instructions.** nano-banana ignored "forearm pointing left" and drifted the framing. gpt-image-2.5-sunburst obeyed it while keeping the base framing. One sample each.
4. **Check where the reveal actually happens before choosing trims.** S01's embroidery only stood at frames 64–76 and first flew at 80–92. A cut at frame 79 would have hidden the story's reveal.
5. **Ops:**
   - The ChatCut plugin removes the computer-use typing cost.
   - Assets download directly from the project storage path.
   - The actual balance is in the editor's account menu → Credits history → Detail. It is not shown by the plugin.
   - At 720p, Seedance 2.5 costs 4.52 for 5 s and 6.31 for 7 s. Image costs 0.6 (nano-banana). ElevenLabs SFX cost 0.18–1.44 each.

## 2026-10-14 — TEST-009 (WEFT director variant): from locked to cinematic
Evidence: experiments/TEST-009/RESULTS.md.
- **Jason's critique of TEST-008:**
  - static camera, two shots that didn't gel, no setup or surprise
  - her beauty was wasted at medium size
  - an odd hand pose, and no music
- **Root cause:** frames mode with first *and* last frames from the same camera locks the camera.
- **Fix:**
  - One anchor per shot (first frame only) plus one eased camera move per shot.
  - 7 shots with deliberate size jumps: wide, beauty close-up, macro tension, eyes, macro reveal, medium flight, close payoff.
  - Music picked first.
- **Keyframe pitfalls:**
  - nano-banana with mixed references produced a different stylised character (K6 a01, flagged by Jason). An edit of an already-good keyframe (sunburst on K1) kept the identity.
  - The extreme-close-up eye keyframe came back mirrored (makeup on the wrong eye), and a local flip fixed it.
- **Ops:** ChatCut allows at most 3 concurrent video jobs (TOO_MANY_JOBS). New ChatCut timelines default to 30 fps.
- **TEST-009 v03 (colour and look continuity):** shots generated from different keyframes carry different worlds: grade, time of day, skin rendering. Jason read the cool, smooth-skinned flight shot as "CG" and "disjoint".
  - Fix at the keyframe: derive neighbouring shots' start frames from the same hero frame (sunburst edit from K7 + K2).
  - Then unify locally: partial LAB transfer to one reference, plus film grain.
  - Watch long orbits: Seedance mirrored her asymmetric makeup and ornaments once the head turned frontal, so trim before the drift.
- **TEST-009 v04 (final):** a grade cannot move a shot into another world.
  - Pushing a cool daylight shot to a warm reference turned it orange.
  - Sky-only tweaks still read as stormy and dark to Jason.
  - What worked was regenerating the *window view* in the keyframe (sunburst edit), then a local window-area LAB match to the reference shots' window statistics.
  - Check colour on the keyframe before paying for video; Jason asked for exactly that.
  - 1080p finish: run Real-ESRGAN on grain-free frames, then add grain after (ESRGAN smears grain).

## 2026-10-16 — film 4 WIND / TEST-010 video lessons (Claude Opus 5.5 + ChatCut plugin; Seedance 2.5, MiniMax H3)
Evidence: experiments/TEST-010/RESULTS.md; delivery/DCL-20261015-WIND-2026-10-16/COMPARISON_FILM01_04.md.
1. **Check mechanical props against real physics before paid video.** Jason caught a hammer hinge that read as an up/down joint for a side-to-side swing. Draw the rig in a design still first (single axle through two bearings, nothing beside the arm, arm rigid into the head), get sign-off, then generate shots. This rig redesign cost about 10 reshoots.
2. **Realism is resolution.** Photoreal stills became "CGI" in video at 720p. Measured fine-texture retention vs the start frame: Seedance 720p 43–78%, MiniMax H3 768p 89–93%, Seedance 1080p 89–95% (2.5× the cost: 11.17 credits per 5 s). Use 1080p for hero and close shots; Real-ESRGAN (free, local) for the rest.
3. **Relight start frames; don't grade CG.** Too-bright/CGI was fixed in the keyframe (low-key window light, matte worn metal, contact shadows), then a filmic grade (`work/look.py`). Check every shot's armour/identity against the C01 sheet; video takes drift (plate armour, younger face).
4. **Sound:** level-normalise every SFX, blend music changes over ~1.4 s (no sudden dips), measure sync against picture motion peaks. Breath SFX must start when the lips purse. Jason finds added title SFX amateur; let the cut land on an existing hit.
5. **Titles:** centre on the optical body of the calligraphy (exclude tails and splatter), keep the English inside the lockup, colour it from the strokes' own gradient, and slam in on a sound already in the mix.
6. **Small physical details break realism.** A static oil drop read as glued on; local VFX (the drop's own pixels, a clean plate, drip on a causal beat, gravity at macro scale) fixed it at 0 credits.
7. **Revisions after approval:** freeze each approved revision, stream-copy the approved audio into new picture revisions (bit-identical), and keep earlier finals as history.
