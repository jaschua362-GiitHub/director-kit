# Still-to-video image quality acceptance
User direction31October2026: preserve the full perceived render quality of the supplied still artwork in the moving film. Repeated softening of skin/detail in video is not an acceptable default.

## What the requirement means
The approved stills are the minimum visual reference for identity, photographic skin, hair, costume, glass/metal and world rendering. Match perceived material/photographic quality at comparable framing, focus, exposure and subject pixel size. “100%” is the user's acceptance target, not a measured percentage or a generator guarantee. Motion changes pixels; do not promise byte-identical video or assign a fake fidelity score.

Director-owned expression, staging, light, lens, depth of field and motivated motion blur remain available. They must not excuse unmotivated waxy faces, erased texture or temporal damage. Fine detail should be present when the intended subject is in focus and remain coherent through motion.

## Before scaling production
1. Verify the approved sources and keep native individual PNGs. Do not condition a face from a screenshot, contact sheet, social download or a compressed review proxy when the original exists.
2. Lock the rough monochrome storyboard first under the existing workflow. Then make purpose-built photographic shot inputs with the same accepted face/material finish. Inspect those inputs before motion; do not propagate a degraded derivative.
3. Use an actual needed film shot as the first representative motion test, within the authorised session budget. Include the difficult face/performance or source-light transition the film really needs; a frozen face with a camera drift is not evidence for a moving performance.
4. Prefer native delivery-resolution detail for face/hero shots. A cheaper low-resolution or upscale route is acceptable only if the actual matched comparison passes. Resolution labels and upscale factors do not prove texture fidelity. This quality requirement overrides an economical route that visibly fails, while existing spending limits remain.
5. Inspect that actual clip before rendering the rest. If the current route cannot retain the look, fix input/reference controls, motion/staging or route within authorised access. Show a bounded failure comparison and seek direction if the gap cannot be resolved within the cap. Never silently lower the visual standard or spend without authorisation.

## Compare actual outputs
At native display size, compare the approved identity anchor AND the actual shot input against decoded original-video frames. Use comparable face/prop crops and downward scale the higher-resolution reference when needed for a fair pixel budget; never claim a tight studio face and a distant video figure have equivalent microdetail. Keep the full composition alongside crops.

Inspect the beginning, middle and end, plus expression/rotation peaks, focus/light transitions and fast movement. Watch the complete clip in real time and inspect denser samples around suspected defects. A contact sheet or one good first frame cannot establish temporal quality.

Reject unmotivated identity/age/proportion drift; porcelain smoothing or wax sheen; skin/iris/hair/fabric detail that melts or boils; frozen pasted facial detail; changing jewellery/strap/pendant geometry; gummy glass/metal; unexplained beauty bloom; sharpening halos; texture noise; banding/blocking or obvious compression softness. Distinguish intended focus/blur/exposure changes from defects with the actual shot context.

Record sources/hashes, actual provider/model/settings, original dimensions/fps, timecodes and comparison files, findings, repair, recheck and pass/hold in the shot ledger. Automated sharpness/similarity metrics may flag changes; they do not certify aesthetic “100%”, and different pose/lighting frames are not ground-truth image pairs.

## Finishing and release
Preserve original provider clips and high-quality intermediates. Avoid repeated lossy recompression. Upscale original used shots rather than the assembled film under existing lab guidance; inspect any face/detail reconstruction rather than assuming it is faithful. Sharpening, grain, blur, denoising or global darkening cannot be used to conceal source degradation.

Check again after upscale, retime/interpolation, grade, grain and final encode. Re-open the exact delivery file at intended playback size and in native crops; compare its clean frames to the source clip/input. Preserve correct skin exposure, coherent highlights and fine texture. The final encode must not introduce a new visible loss.

The director catches and repairs known avoidable degradation before user review. Any unresolved gap is explicit with timecodes and actual comparison evidence; it is not labelled “quality matched”. User approval of a moving result remains separate from source approval. This standard does not authorise paid jobs, production before board/source locks, new tools or publication.
