# Concept art that supports the shot

2026-10-17 — Preparation/handoff improvement from Jason's supplied [X article by AI-刘](https://x.com/LiuLoky50572/status/2111413416309256628) and our existing Lab continuity work. Adapted methods, not a demonstrated new quality result. The project-local XR02 study preserves the inspection/coverage record; this guide is self-contained and does not require those external comparison boards.

## Compose before adding detail

Identify the relationships that make this particular image work: subject scale, face/eye location, camera height, prop position, horizon and useful empty space. When important, record normalised coordinates/fractions or simple annotated measurements in the existing shot map. Judge large shapes and scene relationships before expanding surface detail. Do not manufacture exact measurements from an unseen reference. A director may change composition intentionally; distinguish that choice from accidental drift.

## State what each reference controls

Give actual inputs explicit roles in the asset/shot map: identity and signature styling; material/construction detail; opening pose/state; composition; environment/light; optional end or intermediate state. Mark review collages as review references. A high-detail face, hand or gem image may guide identity/materials while a different frame supplies the correct opening pose. Choose the opening state for its place in the action, not just its sharpness. Use only references supported by the actual production route and approval scope.

## Make change legible

For a consequential action, describe a small number of meaningful phases: preparation, contact, loading/occlusion, reveal/release and settlement, as applicable. Keep actor movement, camera movement and secondary cloth/prop motion distinct. Track prop count, holder/location, visible/hidden areas, support foot/contact, garment/material state and intended ending pose across those phases. Distinguish planned states from observed evidence and leave unseen anatomy unknown.

Prepare an intermediate still only when it resolves a real action/contact/occlusion ambiguity. A beautiful first and last frame can still leave the middle undefined. Do not automatically pin both ends from one locked camera when the planned move needs another reference strategy. This is preparation and directing judgment, not a universal shot count, rigid timeline or guarantee of motion.

## Clean each prompt

Separate shared identity/world invariants, this frame's instantaneous state and the video's ordered action. Read the final complete request before submission. Remove copied instructions from unrelated shots, contradictory material/physics cues and ambiguous camera-versus-subject motion. For example, a rigid glass boundary should not inherit water-splash language; a lighting reference need not dictate a later hand pose. Keep the actual sent text and reference-role mapping for diagnosis. More adjectives are not a substitute for a clear state/action brief.

## Validate the right thing

During scheduled preparation, inspect composition, state, materials, anatomy and continuity in actual stills; record motion as untested. Later, in an already authorised production session, test the difficult motion/contact segment before expanding a sequence where useful. Compare opening, critical middle and exit phases; use matched frame/time comparisons when recreating specified timing, or align by action phase when timing intentionally differs. A successfully exported file does not establish the requested action. Preserve successful regions/takes and isolate a useful next fix.
