## 2026-10-14: direct, don't just avoid errors (lessons from films 1–3)

Read [RETROSPECTIVE_2026-10-14_VIDEO_AGENTS.md](RETROSPECTIVE_2026-10-14_VIDEO_AGENTS.md) before planning shots. Jason rated film 3 "The Unstitched" the best so far: "expanding the still frame into a emotional well acted spot". Its first round failed for being safe and dry.

Director's checklist, before any paid video job:
1. **Arc:** world → beauty → tension/misdirection → reveal → reaction → payoff. Is there a real surprise and a reaction shot?
2. **Coverage:** purpose-built start frames per shot (wide, beauty close-up, macro, eyes, medium, payoff). The approved stills are the world, not the shot list.
3. **Camera:** one anchor (first frame) + one continuous eased camera move per shot. Two endpoints from the same camera lock the camera.
4. **Cuts:** at least two size steps or 30°, each motivated by an action, eyeline or reaction.
5. **Music:** 2–3 beds for Jason to pick by ear; cut to the music; SFX on causes.
6. **Climax:** give the fantasy element an intention, a gesture that communicates.
7. **Look:** neighbouring start frames share one world. Colour, hands and identity pass at full resolution **before** video.
8. **Duration:** let the approved synopsis and art guide the storyboard. Keep about10s when strongest; develop20–30s when narrative benefits, with roughly30s as the upper target. Ordinary coverage/pacing expansion within the approved intent needs no routine reapproval or variant label. Label meaningful creative departures as director variants.

## 2026-10-27 — Film 6 directing lessons, shared with every director agent

## 2026-10-27 — Adopted production rules (workflow v1.2 review)

Jason adopted six rules from the local director review of workflow v1.2 (lab file, not in kit: `reviews/WORKFLOW-v1.2-local-director-2026-10-27/LOCAL_DIRECTOR_REVIEW_v1.2.md`). They are process rules, not creative recipes. Shot count, camera, duration and story remain the director's call. Project-specific locks (Wind the Man's locked pipeline, the Shadow mandate) still take precedence.

1. **Per-shot ledger.** Keep `SHOT_LEDGER.json` in the experiment. Each take records:
   - `shot_id`, `take`, `provider/model`, `job_id`, `created_at`
   - `source_px` (actual WxH, e.g. 1278×720), `fps`, `first_frame`, `last_frame` sources
   - `chained_from` / `chains_to` (shots whose start frame is another shot's end frame)
   - `audio_source` (native / discarded / reference), `sync_points` (measured motion peaks the SFX lock to)
   - `edit_in_f`, `edit_n_f`, `handle_frames`, `finish` (UPSCALE / NATIVE_1080 / RETAKE), `cost` (estimate and actual), `status`
2. **Physics and blocking check before any paid video:** rig and mechanism, floor plan and screen sides, eyelines, door/hinge/prop states, costume asymmetry and light source. This adds to the §8 keyframe-first rules above and does not replace them.
3. **Music and timing early.** Choose a bed or tempo before the first assembly when emotion or rhythm drives shot length. Keep music and SFX stems separate.
4. **One independent critique pass** on the assembled cut, by a different model or a cold re-read. Every review states what it literally watched and heard: real-time playback, frames only, or audio heard or not. Claude keeps the final creative judgment; Jason's approval gates are unchanged.
5. **Any re-render is a new take,** including a 1080p re-render of a liked 720p shot or a draft promotion. Re-check identity, action timing, every shot in its `chains_to`, and SFX sync against the new motion peaks. Film 4's 1080p re-renders needed S7 re-timed and S8 re-chained.
6. **Per-shot upscale from the original clip.** Use [tools/upres_shot.py](../tools/upres_shot.py) on each shot's used range plus handles (default ±12 frames). It writes a time-map JSON. Then assemble at delivery size, grade, and add grain last. Never upscale an assembled lower-resolution picture; `TEST-009/finish_1080.py` is for single-resolution legacy use only.

**Resolution route stays:** 720p production, native 1080p for readable faces and hero costume, local upscale for wides, environments and fast motion. The review's skin-crop test showed upscaling near-native on wides but 67–82% of native fine skin texture on a face close-up.

## 2026-11-07 — Four checks adopted from an outside workflow (Jason: "All four")

Source and comparison: references/process/XR03-eianlu-2118317430665613741/START_HERE.md (lab file, not in kit: `references/process/XR03-eianlu-2118317430665613741/START_HERE.md`). These four were not, and he adopted them on 7 Nov 2026. They are checks, not new gates: each runs inside an existing step and costs a few minutes or one small Sonnet call.

1. **Story risk list at the synopsis** (STORY_FOUNDATION.md, "2026-11-07"). Before the board, mark every beat that uses one of the things AI video does badly: characters crossing paths, small objects handled in the hands, on-screen text, mirrors and reflections, left-right asymmetric features, a lighting change inside one shot, three actions in a row for one character, a high angle that hides a face, more than two or three supporting characters. Each mark is a **cost item**, not a ban: the beat either becomes a start-frame shot, or gets a patch budget in the requirements lock (row 16). A story with many marks is still allowed; it is just priced before it is made.
2. **Frame-pair check before every paid first/last-frame job** (PRODUCTION_PLAYBOOK.md §8, item 11). Run `python -I tools/frame_pair_check.py START.png END.png OUT_DIR` and look at the overlay and the difference map: anything that should stay fixed (walls, props, cup size, sky) must not light up. Then, on the enlarged frames: every object the motion uses is already in the start frame; reactions are set up in the start frame; no two people share a pose; space is left where things land or fly, and headroom above people; one wind direction across the scene; no stray objects or lines.
3. **Supervisor check at every gate** (REQUIREMENTS_LOCK_TEMPLATE.md, step 6).
